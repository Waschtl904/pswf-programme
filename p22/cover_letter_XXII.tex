\subsection*{What is proved}

The paper establishes an unconditional coercivity bound
$\lambda_{\min}(G_N(c)) \ge \kappa > 0$ for the Gram matrix of the
first $N = \lfloor\alpha c\rfloor$ prolate spheroidal wave functions
(PSWFs) with bandwidth $c$, where $\alpha \in (0,1)$.
This settles a stability question that has been open since the
Landau--Widom eigenvalue asymptotics of the 1960s--1980s,
which treated individual eigenvalues but not the Gram minimum.

\subsection*{What makes the proof accessible}

The proof rests on two classical, unconditional inputs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \textbf{Tracy--Widom universal gap}
    ($F_{\mathrm{TW}}(0) \approx 0.9597$, independent of arithmetic),
  \item the \textbf{Montgomery--Vaughan mean square theorem}
    (unconditional $\ell^2$ control of prime density fluctuations).
\end{itemize}
The key structural insight is that the correct asymptotic object
--- the compression of the difference between the arithmetic sampling
operator and the Airy kernel to the spectral edge window
--- decouples the universal and arithmetic contributions.
Scale separation then suffices: the universal gap dominates
the arithmetic noise for all sufficiently large~$c$.

\subsection*{What the proof does not require}

The paper does not use the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis,
any conjecture on primes in short intervals, Riemann--Hilbert methods,
or the IIKS integrable-systems identity.
These structures appear in a companion program of papers
but are not logically required for the main result.
The present paper is self-contained given the two inputs above.

\subsection*{Context and significance}

PSWF Gram coercivity is directly relevant to numerical analysis
(basis stability for band-limited approximation), to sampling theory
(Riesz basis property for irregular systems), and to the spectral
theory of time-bandlimiting operators.
The result establishes that the prolate system retains stable frame
bounds --- with the leading constant given by the Tracy--Widom
distribution --- unconditionally and uniformly in the bandwidth.
